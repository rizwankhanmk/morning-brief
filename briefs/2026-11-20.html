Singapore

Straits Times and Mothership

MRT reliability slips from its record but stays at three times the target

Trains averaged 3.14 million km between delays of more than five minutes over the year to October, down from a record 3.38 million but still well above the one million target. The Circle Line fell most, to 1.97 million, after a platform screen door fault on 3 October. North East Line remains the most reliable.

Straits Times

More than 500 MRT escalators will be refurbished, starting 17 December

The Land Transport Authority says 290 escalators on the North-South, East-West and North East lines will get new mechanical and electrical parts first, with 237 on the Circle Line to follow later. The units are 15 to 30 years old, and each one will be out of action for roughly four to five months.

Mothership

One in five etomidate vape offenders reoffended, so penalties now come faster

Of 1,544 people caught with etomidate vapes between September 2025 and September 2026, 367 were caught again, some three or more times. Since 1 October, repeat offenders are placed on supervision or sent to the drug rehabilitation centre much sooner, face more surprise tests, and those under 16 attend a 24-week rehabilitation programme.

Straits Times

Couple charged with lying about their address to get their daughter into a popular school

A mother, 33, allegedly gave a vice-principal and the authorities a false home address to secure a Phase 2C place in the 2023 Primary One exercise, and the father, 39, is accused of helping her. A gag order protects the girl and the school. Both are expected to plead guilty on 7 January.

Mothership

235 people investigated over scams that cost victims about S$4.2 million

A two-week police operation from 5 to 18 November identified 159 men and 76 women, aged 16 to 93, suspected of acting as scammers or money mules in more than 536 cases. Most involved e-commerce, phishing, job, investment and official-impersonation scams. Money laundering carries up to 10 years' jail and a S$500,000 fine.

Odisha moves nearly three lakh people as Cyclone Akvan nears landfall

The weather office expects Akvan to cross the coast between Puri and Gopalpur around Friday noon, with winds of 90 to 100 kmph. By Thursday evening almost 299,000 people had been moved to safety, 2,523 relief camps had opened and 423 rescue teams were in place, with the Coast Guard on standby.

Speaking at FICCI's annual meeting, the prime minister listed the corporate tax cut from 30 to 22 per cent, simpler returns and lower GST rates, then asked whether companies had hired more staff or raised salaries. Times of India

UN population report puts India at 1.47 billion and urges investment in its young

UNFPA's State of World Population 2026 estimates India's population at 1.47 billion, expected to peak near 1.7 billion in about 40 years. More than one in four Indians are aged 10 to 24, which the agency calls a youth moment worth investing in. China's population is put at 1.41 billion.

Japanese forces will hold live-fire drills at Indian ranges for the first time

At their fourth 2+2 dialogue in Tokyo, Rajnath Singh and S Jaishankar signed a letter of intent with their Japanese counterparts allowing Japan's Self-Defence Forces to train at Indian firing ranges, including with surface-to-ship missiles. Both sides agreed to speed up the arrangements. The previous 2+2 was held in New Delhi in August 2024.

Five stories from the day

UN's top court finds Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

The International Court of Justice ruled by nine votes to six that Myanmar intended to destroy a substantial part of the Rohingya and destroyed evidence to hide it. It is the first time an international court has found a state guilty of genocide. The 2017 crackdown drove more than 700,000 people into Bangladesh.

BBC News

Trump, Xi and Putin sign a peace declaration that leaves out Ukraine and Iran

The statement from the Shenzhen summit says a nuclear war cannot be won and must not be fought, and lists priorities including UN reform, more oil supply and protecting diplomatic immunity. It offers no denuclearisation framework and does not mention the two wars involving its signatories, which European leaders criticised.

The Guardian

Putin promises a 'far more painful' answer to Ukraine's strikes on the Crimean Bridge

The bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea was hit for the third time this week early on Thursday, after a missile and drone attack on Tuesday brought down part of its road section. Speaking after the Shenzhen summit, Putin insisted the bridge carries only civilian traffic, which Kyiv disputes.

BBC News

Venezuela signs a military deal with the US less than a year after Maduro's capture

Interim president Delcy Rodriguez signed a defence cooperation agreement with US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth in Caracas, restoring military ties that Hugo Chavez cut in 2005. Few details have been released. Hegseth said Venezuela had gone from the region's most aggressive threat to a compliant security partner.

BBC News

US-led group beats Chinese bidders to Kenya's US$60 billion Mrima Hill minerals project

Frontier Minerals, a consortium of three American firms, won the tender ahead of seven rivals, including Chinese and Australian companies. The site holds niobium and other critical minerals used in electric vehicles and defence. The group promises to process and refine in Kenya, as Washington tries to loosen China's grip on African supply.

Data released by the House Foreign Affairs Committee shows Commerce licensed 879,000 chips between December 2025 and May 2026, including 150,000 for Tencent, 75,000 for Alibaba and 43,000 for Baidu, out of 3.4 million requested.
