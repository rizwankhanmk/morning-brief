From Apple

Apple HomeView went on sale on 13 November at Apple Stores, online and in the Apple Store app in selected countries. The 7-inch home display runs Siri AI, the new conversational Siri, and can recognise up to six people by face to show each their own messages, calendar and reminders. It also handles FaceTime, music, home cameras and hands-free automations.

9to5Mac

Epic asks the Supreme Court to keep Apple's contempt ruling in place

Epic Games has filed its formal reply to Apple's Supreme Court appeal over App Store payment links. A lower court found Apple broke an earlier order by charging a 27 per cent fee on outside purchases and limiting link buttons. Epic says Apple deliberately worked around the order; the justices agreed in June to hear the case.

9to5Mac

Apple explains Secure Exclaves, its walled-off system for always-on sensing

Apple published security research on Secure Exclaves, small separate operating systems that process camera and microphone data for features like HomeView's person recognition and Siri Recap. Rather than passing that constant stream to the main operating system, the exclaves handle it in isolation, much as the Secure Enclave already does for passwords and biometrics.

Apple representatives met Thai officials this week about the threat notifications it sent on 29 October to users in 99 countries, including Thai political figures. Straits Times and Mothership

Cyber agency tells every organisation to tighten defences as incidents triple

The Cyber Security Agency says reported incidents have tripled since September, with at least 12 hitting Singapore organisations or their suppliers between 24 October and 13 November, six of them in healthcare. Attackers are using AI to move faster. It urged patching, multi-factor logins and monitoring, and offered small firms outsourced security chiefs.

Straits Times

Longer traineeships for graduates as retrenchments reach a post-pandemic high

Acting Manpower Minister Jasmin Lau says retrenchments rose to about 4,800 in the third quarter, the most since the pandemic, and hiring is slowing. The ministry plans longer, structured traineeships for fresh graduates and wants to widen support for mid-career workers and protections for professionals, without waiting for the trend to settle.

Straits Times

LTA plans road cameras that spot crashes, wrong-way drivers and illegal parking

A new intelligent video platform will watch at least 960 traffic camera feeds and automatically flag stalled vehicles, debris, fires, floods and people on expressways. It will also help work out what caused an accident and predict hotspots. The Land Transport Authority is testing a similar system for illegal parking and roadwork safety.

Straits Times

SIA's stake in Air India loses a third of its book value in six months

Singapore Airlines wrote its Air India holding down by S$334.7 million between March and September, to S$799.9 million, as it absorbed its share of the airline's losses. The reported loss share fell to S$361.7 million, but only because old provisions were reversed. Chief executive Goh Choon Phong remains upbeat about India.

US grounds every departing flight after an air traffic communications failure

The FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop on Friday after what Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy called a communication problem affecting all en-route control centres. More than 11,000 flights were delayed or cancelled. Homeland security officials said there was no sign of an attack, and the priority was landing planes already in the air.

Tanker strikes a docked river cruise ship in New Orleans

The Atlantic Asphalt tanker hit American Cruise Lines' American Heritage on the Mississippi on Friday afternoon while about 129 passengers were disembarking, leaving the vessel on its side and people in the water. After a large rescue effort, officials later said all passengers and crew had been accounted for alive.
