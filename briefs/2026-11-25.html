Once Siri AI is switched on in iOS 27 and iPadOS 27, swiping down from the middle of the top edge summons Siri rather than Notification Center, ending a habit dating back to iOS 5 in 2011. Notifications now live behind a swipe from the top-left corner, and alerts animate in from there.

StarHub and M1 promise no mobile price changes until their merger closes

Plans from StarHub, M1 and sub-brands such as giga, Circles.Life and MyRepublic will stay at current prices through 2027 until the S$1.1 billion all-share merger is approved, expected in the first half of next year. Singaporean wanted in corruption case caught in Malaysia after four years

A Singaporean man in his 30s, allegedly linked to a local corruption case, was arrested in Malaysia on 22 November after entering the country illegally. He is suspected of bribing Malaysian officers to avoid action over his stay. He was handed to Singapore's CPIB in Johor Bahru the next day.

Mothership

Aware study finds abuse often continues after victims leave

In interviews with 44 survivors of coercive control, Aware found that 33 of the 38 who tried to separate kept facing abuse afterwards, about 87 per cent. Tactics included withheld maintenance, constant messages, using children as leverage and dragging out divorce cases. The 70-page study was released on 25 November.

Straits Times

Malay/Muslim students from households with per capita income of S$1,000 or less will each get S$250 in e-vouchers for textbooks, devices and transport under the new Ready for School Scheme. It is paid per child, from primary school to a first degree. Applications open on 1 December and close on 28 February.

US set to propose tariffs on India and 15 others over excess capacity

The US Trade Representative is about to publish findings from an eight-month Section 301 probe into alleged industrial overcapacity in 16 economies, including India, China and the EU. Hearings would follow before final tariffs in about 45 days. New Delhi says it has no structural excess capacity and expects little impact.

The road transport ministry is working on linking premiums to violations recorded in the previous year, alongside single-premium multi-year policies for cars and two-wheelers. US jury convicts Huawei of trade-secret theft but clears it of racketeering

After a two-month trial in Brooklyn, jurors found Huawei guilty on 10 of 12 counts, including conspiracy to steal trade secrets, bank fraud and sanctions violations, but rejected the headline racketeering charge. Huawei said the acquittal vindicated its position. The charges date back to 2019, after Meng Wanzhou's arrest in Canada.

The former French president and his wife will be tried next year over how a key witness in the Libyan campaign-funding case, the late Ziad Takieddine, withdrew his accusations in 2020. Sarkozy, who served 20 days of a five-year sentence in that case, denies wrongdoing; his appeal verdict is due next Monday.

Putin said Kyiv was ignoring Trump's request to stop striking Russian oil plants and that a response was inevitable. Kyiv says it will stop only when Moscow does. At least 23 people were hurt when a car packed with 80 to 100kg of explosives hit the state government building in Meta province on Monday night. President Abelardo de la Espriella blamed dissident commander Antonio Medina, saying it was revenge for army offensives, and vowed to hunt him down.

Al Jazeera

Landmine casualties hit a five-year high, led by Myanmar and Syria

The Landmine Monitor 2026 report counted 6,929 casualties from mines and unexploded ordnance in 2025, 84 per cent of them civilians and half of those children. Myanmar topped the list for a third year with 1,891 deaths, while Syria's toll rose by more than half as displaced people returned home.

UK cyber agency reports a marked rise in AI-driven attacks

Britain's National Cyber Security Centre says several intrusions since September used autonomous hacking tools, often free open-source penetration testers, to exploit old weaknesses at speed. None of the flaws were new; most came from years of technical debt. Its advice is that basic security hygiene would have stopped many of them.

With grid connections taking up to a decade, US developers have announced over 100 on-site gas power plants for data centres, with more than 115 gigawatts of planned capacity, over double California's peak demand. Few are built yet, but the trend could shape the grid and emissions for decades.
