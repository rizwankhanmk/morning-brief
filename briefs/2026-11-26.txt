EXPLAINER

What key man risk is, and why a credit agency cares about one CEO
Key man risk is the danger that a company depends so heavily on one person that losing them would damage the business. It is usually a worry for small firms. S&P has now applied it to Nvidia, a company worth trillions, after Jensen Huang resigned and was reinstated within eleven days with no successor in place.
Why would a credit agency care? A rating measures how likely a borrower is to repay. S&P says Nvidia's finances are strong, but weak board oversight and no succession plan make the future less predictable. A cut from AA to AA- would barely touch Nvidia itself. It matters because Nvidia guarantees loans for data-centre builders that cannot borrow cheaply alone, so their costs rise with its rating.
The takeaway for governance: a board's job includes planning for the day the founder is gone. When it has not, lenders price that gap in, and so does everyone who borrows on its name.
