Models and products

DeepSeek open-sources an internal framework for running models cheaply

DeepSeek has released DeepInfer, the inference engine behind its own services, under an Apache 2.0 licence. It runs DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on Nvidia's newest GPUs and Huawei's Ascend 950 chips and was built largely on vLLM. A senior researcher framed it as part of a push for open models to win outright; the speed claims are DeepSeek's own.

South China Morning Post

Google joins Amazon in helping PyTorch developers leave Nvidia GPUs

Google has open-sourced TorchTPU, which lets existing PyTorch code run on its TPUs with a configuration change rather than a rewrite, days after Amazon did the same for Trainium with TorchNeuron. An agent suite helps port apps and fix errors. Both target Nvidia's CUDA software moat, though performance on the new chips is unproven.

Policy and chips

Anthropic pitches an AI safety bill with a judge-approval step

Anthropic's draft federal bill, first reported by Politico, would let the government block a frontier model only for a foreseeable, imminent risk of more than US$1 billion in damage or 50 or more deaths, and only after the Commerce Department persuades a judge in Washington. No agency could act alone. Critics say the bar is far too high.

TechCrunch

China sets up a state-owned firm in Xiongan to manage national data for AI

China National Data Group, launched in Xiongan on Monday as the 100th centrally run state enterprise, is backed by the big telecoms and power utilities and overseen by SASAC. Its job is to pool national data resources, break down regional and industry silos, and secure a supply of training data for domestic AI models.
